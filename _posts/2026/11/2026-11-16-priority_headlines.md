# Strategic Overview

## 1. Two unrelated failures exposed the same dependency
- **The FAA ground stop and the Microsoft outage overlapped but were not linked**: officials ruled out cyberattack on both, and the aviation cause was pushed toward contractor L3Harris and an aging system.
- **The political framing is now the story**: the Transportation Secretary warned the system could fail again, which converts a one-day ground stop into an infrastructure-funding argument.
- **Cloud and aviation remain the two chokepoints that convert a control-plane fault into a national service interruption**, even when no adversary is involved.

## 2. The Houthi campaign has shifted from Yemen to Saudi energy geography
- **Three distinct target sets appeared in one batch**: Riyadh and military sites, Eastern Province, and the Yanbu oil facility, with Aramco attacks claimed separately.
- **The diplomatic track is running in parallel**: the UNSC extended sanctions, Riyadh asked the EU to designate the Houthis, and Riyadh–Beijing talks on the Houthi file were reported.
- **Saudi Arabia turning to Syria for troops** signals a manpower problem in air and missile defence rather than a new offensive posture.

## 3. Iran–US escalation is maritime and economic before it is kinetic
- **The Bahrain base drone attack and the Hormuz tanker explosions** are the two operational edges of the same escalation, and both were answered with intercepts rather than confirmed hits.
- **The US blockade of Iranian oil exports** is doing more to shape the market picture than any single strike, and it raises the value of every Houthi disruption to Saudi output.
- **Protective posture inside the US is visible**: intercepted Iranian drone plotting led to scaled-back public appearances and dispersal of the line of succession, which is a continuity signal rather than a CI incident.

## 4. Russia's energy war is now attrition against refining, not just exports
- **A ninth refinery struck since 1 November**, framed against 30% of Russian oil-processing capacity, marks a sustained campaign against downstream assets rather than one-off export terminals.
- **The Moscow car bombings, two in two days and one targeting a former Ukrainian PM's aide**, keep the assassination track active inside Russia alongside the energy strikes.
- **Neither track produced a new confirmed infrastructure outage in these cards**; the operational effect is being asserted by the outlets, not measured.

## 5. Espionage and insider risk is the densest theme in the batch, and mostly non-operational
- **The Nvidia/Jensen Huang case generated a dozen cards but no new intrusion**: congressional summons, Singapore's denial, and US intelligence doubts about the Alice Huang evidence all point to a case under stress rather than expanding.
- **The substantive counterintelligence items are small and specific**: two IRGC spy convictions in the UK, the named US Army officer held over a NATO base, and Austria's new espionage law.
- **Corporate response is now a market of its own**, with former spooks hired at reported $500,000 packages and counterintelligence training pitched to CEOs; that is posture, not incident.

## 6. The domestic CI baseline stayed local and short-duration
- **No single nationwide emergency dominated**: outages, gas and fuel responses, a hospital battery injury, and a chemical-plant report were all local exclusion-zone events.
- **The one sustained US security incident was at a campus-adjacent media facility**, Howard University's WHUR/WHUT complex, where the trigger explanation shifted from shooting to bomb threat during the day.
- **The protest pipeline is mostly forward-dated**: Washington state recurring highway and federal-facility actions, plus Mexican agricultural actions including a five-state event on 17 November.
