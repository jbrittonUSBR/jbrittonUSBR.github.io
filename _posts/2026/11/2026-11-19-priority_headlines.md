# Strategic Overview

## 1. The Gulf exchange has gone kinetic and bilateral
- **A US strike on an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island was followed by Iranian strikes in Bahrain and Kuwait**, with Iran claiming US personnel injured — a shift from the interception-and-denial pattern briefed on 17 and 18 November.
- **The target set is sensing and basing**: a missile site, a US base and Gulf positions, extending pressure beyond the single Bahrain radar claim carried earlier.
- **What it means**: the risk of miscalculation rises when both sides strike named territory; the operational effect this cycle is a wider Gulf posture rather than a confirmed shutdown of any asset.

## 2. Iran's threat narrative keeps outrunning the evidence
- **The dirty-bomb story persisted across many cards but softened at the top**: Rubio said there was "no imminent threat," a senior US official called the reporting "not new," and a congressman noted such threats arrive "quite often."
- **Tehran denied the plot and the Iranian embassy in Athens dismissed the assassination allegations**, keeping attribution contested.
- **What it means**: no device, location or timeline has surfaced; the operative effect remains rhetorical and posture-driven around Washington.

## 3. Houthi pressure shifted from chokepoint control to Riyadh's air defence
- **The coalition intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at the Riyadh region** across three cards, following the already-briefed claims on Riyadh airport and a financial centre.
- **The Red Sea chokepoint and Saudi commander dismissal are established facts from earlier batches**, not new this cycle.
- **What it means**: Riyadh is under periodic missile pressure while Houthi control of Bab el-Mandeb remains the standing maritime risk; the new fact is the interception, not a fresh territorial gain.

## 4. Ukraine is widening the target set inside Russia
- **Two oil refineries deep inside Russia and an underground gas pipeline used for a sneak attack** add energy and pipeline nodes to the Crimea bridge strikes already briefed.
- **What it means**: the campaign is reaching further into Russian energy infrastructure and using non-air routes, raising the difficulty of point defence even though the bridge remains the symbolic centre of gravity.

## 5. The cyber continuity story is a contest over attribution
- **New named targets appeared**: Japan's Fugaku supercomputer, Equinor, a Visa/JPMorgan ransomware claim and an Irish credit union.
- **The outage narrative was publicly cleared of attack**: Apple blamed an internal update, and Rubio and the national intelligence director saw no evidence of a coordinated cyberattack behind the "178 websites" disruption and the NSA network outage.
- **What it means**: control-plane faults and vendor reach dominate the continuity picture, with adversaries asserted more often than confirmed.

## 6. Domestic disruption was weather and load, not adversaries
- **California's PG&E footprint exceeded 10,000 customers across 37 counties** alongside flooding in San Luis Obispo; Georgia Power exceeded 8,000 across 19 counties and Entergy more than 3,000 across four states.
- **The Attalla industrial fire forced a one-mile evacuation**, and a power blip in Spartanburg cascaded into potable-water disruption.
- **What it means**: the near-term domestic burden is restoration and protective action, with no hostile cause established in the alert reporting.
