# Strategic Overview

## 1. The FBI and USDA breaches became the dominant US cyber story
- **The national crime database was reportedly hacked, prompting an FBI investigation** described as a second attack in months, while **USDA systems used to pay farmers were knocked offline for days**.
- **A federal report raised concern about cyberattacks on the US food supply**, and political figures publicly reframed the FBI incident, with Trump suggesting it could expose a supposed "Russia hoax."
- **What it means**: the concrete effects are a payment-system outage and a government database compromise, while the public narrative is splitting along partisan lines; no confirmed data loss or attribution appeared in the cards.

## 2. The Houthi campaign shifted back toward shipping and port coercion
- **The Houthis ordered ships to leave Saudi Red Sea ports after Hodeidah airstrikes**, and both sides exchanged threats to bomb key ports.
- **Coalition intercepts of missiles headed for Riyadh continued**, while market commentary put Brent near $93 on supply concerns; the Riyadh tower strike itself was already briefed.
- **What it means**: the pressure point is moving from symbolic strikes on financial towers toward maritime access and port infrastructure, with energy prices as the transmission channel.

## 3. Iran's confrontational rhetoric hardened alongside new attack claims
- **The IRGC chief said Iran was entering a "final confrontation" with the US**, and **Trump warned Iran over a reported dirty-bomb plot**, calling it a rumour.
- **The IRGC downed a US drone over Hormuz**, and Iran launched a missile-drone attack on US bases in Bahrain reported to have hit Patriot radars; Iran dismissed the dirty-bomb reporting as fabricated.
- **What it means**: the exchange remains claim-and-counterclaim around named military assets and drones, with no confirmed infrastructure loss.

## 4. Russia-linked assassination and espionage allegations spread across Europe
- **Denmark said Russia was behind a plot to kill a US-Ukrainian citizen on its soil and moved to expel a Russian diplomat** over an alleged plot against a Ukrainian rapper.
- **Cyprus expelled Russian diplomats on suspicion of espionage**, and von der Leyen said Russian threats were becoming "less and less hybrid" after the Denmark plot.
- **What it means**: the pattern is expulsions and attributions rather than incidents on infrastructure, keeping the sabotage-risk narrative alive without a named CI target.

## 5. Drone and missile strikes kept hitting maritime and urban targets
- A suspected drone strike damaged a Russian vessel at a Libyan port and another Russian cargo ship burned in the Baltic off Finland.
- A Russian drone strike collapsed an apartment building in the Kyiv region with people feared trapped, and air-raid sirens sounded in Kaliningrad.
- **What it means**: the strikes show continued UAS reach against ships and urban structures, with attribution resting on officials and state media rather than independent confirmation.

## 6. Mass-casualty violence stayed on soft targets
- Shootings at a California private concert and an Ohio baby shower were reported, and an IS-linked ADF attack in eastern Congo was reported to have killed at least 141 civilians.
- A drone strike collapsed a Kyiv apartment building, and the outlet-reported Bahrain base attack fit the same pattern of attacks on crowded or unprotected sites.
- **What it means**: no critical-infrastructure asset was the stated target; the recurring exposure is crowded civilian venues.
